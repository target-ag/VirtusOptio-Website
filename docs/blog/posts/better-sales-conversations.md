# Better sales conversations start with better questions

A sales conversation does not need to begin with a pitch. It can begin with curiosity: what is happening in the customer’s business, what are they trying to change, and why does it matter now?

When you make room for those answers, you have a better basis for deciding whether your help is relevant. You also give the other person space to think, without asking them to commit before the problem is clear.

<!-- more -->

## Start with their situation

Before describing your services, ask the customer to walk you through how things work today. Stay close to their examples instead of moving straight to your interpretation.

A few useful starting points:

- What prompted you to have this conversation?
- How are you handling this at the moment?
- Where does the current approach become difficult?

If an answer feels broad, ask for a recent example. “Follow-up is inconsistent” becomes easier to understand when you know what happened after the last customer meeting.

## Explore what needs to change

Once you understand the situation, explore the impact. The aim is to understand the customer’s priorities, not to make their problem sound bigger than it is.

Ask what the issue affects, who is involved, and what an improvement would look like. A customer might care most about time, clarity, customer experience, or a more predictable process. Let them tell you which matters.

> “If we were having this conversation again in a few months, what would you want to be different?”

That question can reveal the outcome they actually want. It also helps separate an immediate need from something that would simply be nice to have.

## Check your understanding before offering a solution

Summarise the situation in plain language and invite a correction. For example: “It sounds as though the biggest issue is keeping track of the next step after a good first conversation. Have I understood that correctly?”

Only then connect your service to the problem. Explain what you could help with, what would be outside the scope, and what you still need to learn. Avoid promising an outcome you cannot yet support.

## Agree on a useful next step

A next step should have a purpose for both people. It might be sharing an example, involving a colleague, or taking a closer look at the current process. It does not always need to be a proposal.

Make the agreement specific:

1. **What will happen?** Name the action and what it should clarify.
2. **Who will do it?** Give each action an owner.
3. **When will you reconnect?** Choose timing that works for both sides.

If there is no clear fit, acknowledge it. Leaving someone with clarity can be more valuable than pushing the conversation toward a decision they are not ready to make.

## Build a habit you can repeat

After the call, write a short note about the customer’s situation, desired outcome, and agreed next step. Keep it simple enough to use consistently.

The purpose of a practical sales system is to support your attention, not replace it. A few thoughtful questions and a clear follow-up can make the next conversation easier to prepare for and more useful to the customer.
